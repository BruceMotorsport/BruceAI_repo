## 5. Sensors — Pressure, Temperature, Flow Rate

### 5.1 Pressure Sensor
|- **What it does**: Measures hydraulic pressure for monitoring and control |
|- **How it works**: Piezoresistive or piezoelectric — voltage proportional to pressure |
|- **How to test**: Multimeter → mV output; compare to pressure gauge |
|- **Standard running data**: 0-400 bar (typical industrial) |
|- **Thresholds**: Low <10 bar, High >450 bar |
|- **Failure modes**: Damaged diaphragm, wiring fault, ECU fault |

### 5.2 Temperature Sensor
|- **What it does**: Measures fluid temperature for thermal management |
|- **How it works**: Thermistor or RTD — resistance changes with temperature |
|- **How to test**: Multimeter → resistance; compare to temperature chart |
|- **Standard running data**: 30-80°C (operating), 100°C (max) |
|- **Thresholds**: Low <10°C, High >100°C |
|- **Failure modes**: Broken sensor, wiring short, ECU fault |

### 5.3 Flow Sensor
|- **What it does**: Measures fluid flow rate for metering |
|- **How it works**: Turbine, paddle wheel, or ultrasonic — pulse output |
|- **How to test**: Pulse counter; compare to flow meter |
|- **Standard running data**: 0-100 L/min (typical) |
|- **Thresholds**: Low <0.5 L/min, High >120 L/min |
|- **Failure modes**: Turbine jam, air bubble, wiring fault |

### 5.4 Level Sensor
|- **What it does**: Measures reservoir fluid level |
|- **How it works**: Float, capacitive, or ultrasonic |
|- **How to test**: Visual check; sensor output |
|- **Standard running data**: 0-100% |
|- **Thresholds**: Low <10%, High >95% |
|- **Failure modes**: Float stuck, capacitor drift |

### 5.5 Filter Differential Pressure Sensor
|- **What it does**: Measures filter condition (ΔP across filter) |
|- **How it works**: Piezoresistive — pressure differential |
|- **How to test**: Compare ΔP to filter spec |
|- **Standard running data**: 0-5 bar (ΔP) |
|- **Thresholds**: >3 bar (replace filter) |
|- **Failure modes**: Sensor failure, bypass valve stuck |

## 6. Actuators — Control Valves, Proportional, Servo

### 6.1 Proportional Valve
|- **What it does**: Modulates fluid flow proportionally to input signal |
|- **How it works**: Solenoid + spool → proportional flow |
|- **How to test**: Current → flow output; compare to spec |
|- **Standard running data**: 4-20 mA or 0-10 V input |
|- **Thresholds**: Low <3.5 mA, High >21 mA |
|- **Failure modes**: Spool stuck, coil burn, wiring fault |

### 6.2 Servo Valve
|- **What it does**: High-precision flow control for position/force |
|- **How it works**: Torque motor + spool → zero-lap control |
|- **How to test**: Command → position feedback; compare to spec |
|- **Standard running data**: ±10 mA command |
|- **Thresholds**: >±20% deviation |
|- **Failure modes**: Spool fatigue, nozzle clog, coil burn |

### 6.3 Pressure-Compensated Valve
|- **What it does**: Maintains constant pressure despite flow changes |
|- **How it works**: Spool + pressure spring → compensated flow |
|- **How to test**: Pressure gauge → constant pressure at varying flow |
|- **Standard running data**: Set pressure ±5% |
|- **Thresholds**: >±10% deviation |
|- **Failure modes**: Spool wear, spring fatigue, orifice clog |

## 7. Theory of Operation — Pascal's Law, Bernoulli, Flow

### 7.1 Pascal's Law
```
Pressure applied to confined fluid transmits equally in all directions.

P = F / A
P = pressure (Pa or bar)
F = force (N)
A = area (m²)

Example:
Force = 100 N, Area = 0.01 m²
Pressure = 100 / 0.01 = 10,000 Pa = 1 bar
```

### 7.2 Bernoulli's Principle
```
P + ½ρv² + ρgh = constant

P = static pressure
ρ = fluid density
v = fluid velocity
g = gravitational acceleration
h = height

Application: Venturi effect — velocity increases, pressure decreases.
```

### 7.3 Flow Rate
```
Q = A × v

Q = flow rate (L/min or m³/s)
A = cross-sectional area (m²)
v = velocity (m/s)

Example:
A = 0.001 m², v = 2 m/s
Q = 0.001 × 2 = 0.002 m³/s = 7.2 L/min
```

### 7.4 Power
```
P = Q × ΔP

P = hydraulic power (W or kW)
Q = flow rate (m³/s)
ΔP = pressure differential (Pa)

Example:
Q = 0.002 m³/s, ΔP = 10,000,000 Pa
P = 0.002 × 10,000,000 = 20,000 W = 20 kW
```

### 7.5 Efficiency
```
η = (Output Power) / (Input Power) × 100%

Pump: η = (Q × ΔP_out) / (Torque × ω) × 100%
Motor: η = (Torque × ω) / (Q × ΔP_in) × 100%
```

## 8. Diagnostic — Pressure Testing, Flow Measurement, Leak Detection

### 8.1 Pressure Testing Procedure
1. **Connect pressure gauge** to test port (P, A, or B)
2. **Start pump** at rated speed
3. **Record pressure** at no-flow condition
4. **Apply load** and record pressure drop
5. **Compare** to specification

### 8.2 Flow Measurement Procedure
1. **Install flow meter** in line (portable)
2. **Start system** at rated operating conditions
3. **Record flow rate** at various speeds
4. **Compare** to pump displacement × RPM
5. **Calculate** volumetric efficiency

### 8.3 Leak Detection Methods
| Method | Sensitivity | Cost | Application |
|--------|-------------|------|-------------|
| Visual | — | Free | External leaks |
| Soap solution | Medium | Low | External leaks |
| Dye + UV | Medium | Medium | External leaks |
| Pressure decay | High | Medium | Internal leaks |
| Acoustic | High | High | Internal/external |
| Oil particle counter | Very High | Very High | Internal contamination |

### 8.4 Common Diagnostic Codes
| Code | Description | Cause |
|------|-------------|-------|
| P1xxx | Hydraulic pressure fault | Pump, valve, sensor |
| P2xxx | Flow rate fault | Sensor, flow meter |
| P3xxx | Temperature fault | Cooler, sensor |
| P4xxx | Level fault | Sensor, reservoir |
| P5xxx | Filter fault | Clogged, bypass |

## 9. Service Procedures — Fluid Analysis, Filter Service, Pump Rebuild

### 9.1 Fluid Analysis Procedure
1. **Sample fluid** from drain port (hot, running system)
2. **Visual inspection**: Color, clarity, odor
3. **Lab analysis**: Viscosity, moisture, particle count, TAN/TBN
4. **Compare** to ISO/AGMA standards
5. **Decision**: Reuse, filter, or replace

### 9.2 Filter Service Procedure
1. **Stop system** and depressurize
2. **Remove filter** element
3. **Inspect** housing for contamination
4. **Install** new filter element
5. **Refill** if necessary
6. **Run** and verify pressure drop

### 9.3 Pump Rebuild Procedure
1. **Disassemble** pump carefully
2. **Inspect** all components (wear, scoring, corrosion)
3. **Measure** clearances (gears, pistons, vanes)
4. **Replace** seals, bearings, worn parts
5. **Reassemble** with clean fluid
6. **Test** pressure, flow, efficiency

## 10. Appendix — Non-Conversant Folk Reference

### 10.1 What Is Pascal's Law?
Pressure applied to a confined fluid transmits equally in all directions. Used in hydraulic jacks, presses, and systems.

### 10.2 What Is Bernoulli's Principle?
In a flowing fluid, increased velocity correlates with decreased pressure. Applied in venturi meters and nozzle design.

### 10.3 What Is Hydraulic Pressure?
Force per unit area transmitted by an incompressible fluid. Measured in bar or psi. Standard industrial: 200-350 bar.

### 10.4 What Is Flow Rate?
Volume of fluid passing a point per unit time. Measured in L/min or m³/s. Typical: 0.5-100 L/min.

### 10.5 What Is a Gear Pump?
Positive displacement pump using meshing gears. Reliable, low cost. Max pressure: 200-350 bar.

### 10.6 What Is a Piston Pump?
Positive displacement pump using reciprocating pistons. High pressure, variable flow. Max pressure: 350-500 bar.

### 10.7 What Is a Hydraulic Cylinder?
Linear actuator converting hydraulic pressure to linear force. Bore sizes: 25-300 mm.

### 10.8 What Is a Hydraulic Motor?
Rotary actuator converting hydraulic pressure to rotational torque. Types: gear, piston, vane.

### 10.9 What Is a Directional Control Valve?
Controls fluid direction to actuators. Common types: 4/3 (4 ports, 3 positions).

### 10.10 What Is a Proportional Valve?
Solenoid-controlled valve providing proportional flow control. Input: 4-20 mA.

### 10.11 What Is a Servo Valve?
High-precision valve using torque motor. Used for position/force control.

### 10.12 What Is ISO VG?
International Standards Organization Viscosity Grade. Common: ISO VG 32 (28-40 cSt at 40°C).

### 10.13 What Is a Pressure Sensor?
Piezoresistive device measuring hydraulic pressure. Output: mV/V or 4-20 mA.

### 10.14 What Is a Flow Sensor?
Turbine or ultrasonic device measuring fluid flow. Output: pulses or 4-20 mA.

### 10.15 What Is a Filter Differential Sensor?
Measures pressure drop across a filter to indicate filter condition. Threshold: >3 bar.
