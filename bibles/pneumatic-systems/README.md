## 7. Sensors — Pressure, Position, Flow

### 7.1 Pressure Sensor (Pneumatic)
|- **What it does**: Measures compressed air pressure |
|- **How it works**: Diaphragm + strain gauge, mV output |
|- **How to test**: Multimeter → mV; compare to pressure gauge |
|- **Standard running data**: 0-10 bar (typical) |
|- **Thresholds**: Low <0.5 bar, High >12 bar |
|- **Failure modes**: Diaphragm rupture, wiring fault, ECU fault |

### 7.2 Position Sensor
|- **What it does**: Measures actuator position |
|- **How it works**: Reed switch, potentiometer, LVDT |
|- **How to test**: Multimeter → continuity at positions |
|- **Standard running data**: 0-100% of stroke |
|- **Thresholds**: Incomplete switch, erratic reading |
|- **Failure modes**: Magnet loss, switch weld, wear |

### 7.3 Flow Sensor
|- **What it does**: Measures air flow rate |
|- **How it works**: Turbine, thermal mass, ultrasonic |
|- **How to test**: Flow meter; compare to pump curve |
|- **Standard running data**: 0-500 L/min |
|- **Thresholds**: Flow <rated, high pressure drop |
|- **Failure modes**: Blockage, sensor drift, wiring |

## 9. Theory of Operation — Boyle's Law, Ideal Gas Law

### 9.1 Boyle's Law (Pressure-Volume)
```
P₁ × V₁ = P₂ × V₂ (constant temperature)

Example:
P₁ = 1 bar, V₁ = 10 L
P₂ = 10 bar, V₂ = 1 bar
```

### 9.2 Charles' Law (Temperature-Volume)
```
V₁/T₁ = V₂/T₂ (constant pressure)

T in Kelvin
```

### 9.3 Ideal Gas Law
```
PV = nRT

P = Pressure (kPa, bar)
V = Volume (m³, L)
n = moles of gas
R = gas constant
T = Temperature (K)
```

### 9.4 Polytropic Process
```
PVⁿ = constant

n = polytropic index
n = 1.0 (isothermal)
n = 1.4 (adiabatic, diatomic gas)
```

## 10. Diagnostic — Pressure Testing, Leak Detection, Flow Measurement

### 10.1 Pressure Testing Procedure
1. Connect pressure gauge to test port
2. Start compressor, pressurize system
3. Isolate section, monitor pressure decay
4. Allow @>15% drop = leak detected
5. Use soap solution to locate leaks

### 10.2 Leak Detection Methods
| Method | Sensitivity | Cost |
|--------|-------------|------|
| Visual Soap | Medium | Low |
| Electronic | High | High |
| Ultrasonic | Very High | Very High |
| Pressure Decay | High | Medium |

| Orifice Plate | ±1-3% | Large ducts |
| Venturi | ±1-2% | Clean air |

## 12. Appendix — Non-Conversant Folk Reference

### 12.1 What Is Compressed Air?
Air pressurized to >1 bar for work (pneumatic tools, actuators).

### 12.2 What Is a Pneumatic Cylinder?
Linear actuator converting air pressure to linear force.

### 12.3 What Is a 4/3 Valve?
Directional valve with 4 ports and 3 positions (center, extend, retract).

### 12.4 What Is a FRL?
Filter, Regulator, Lubricator — integrated air preparation unit.

### 12.5 What Is a Dew Point?
Temperature where water vapor condenses. Refrigerated: ~7°C, Desiccant: <-40°C.

### 12.6 What Is Boyle's Law?
Pressure x Volume = constant at steady temperature.

### 12.7 What Is an Air Dryer?
Removes moisture from compressed air (refrigerated or desiccant).

### 12.8 What Is a Pressure Relief Valve?
Safety valve releasing excess pressure (set 6-10 bar).

### 12.9 What Is a Solenoid Valve?
Electromagnetic valve controlled by 12V or 24V DC.

### 12.10 What Is Flow Rate?
Volume or mass per unit time (L/min, SCFM, m³/min).

### 12.11 What Is Filter Coalescing?
Removes oil and water droplets from air.

### 12.12 What Is a Rotary Vane Pump?
Positive displacement compressor with rotating vanes.

### 12.13 What Is a Scroll Compressor?
Oil-free, twin spirals compress air.

### 12.14 What Is Pipe Sizing?
Size for flow velocity 20-30 m/s, pressure drop <10%.

### 12.15 What Is Pipe Clamps?
Support pipes every 1-2 meters, avoid over-tightening.
